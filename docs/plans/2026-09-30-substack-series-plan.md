## Ready-to-edit Notes

These are original proposed Note drafts. Add the relevant source URL after checking the live article. They remain local for review.

**Reintroduction**

The Why Chromosome is returning to a question that has kept changing shape: what happens when ancient disciplines of attention meet the systems we build today? I’m beginning a series on speech, memory, and witness—drawn from Synchronocities. The first movement begins in the mouth, before it reaches the machine.

**1A — Speech**

A promise changes the room before anyone moves. Language can carry information, but it can also alter what becomes possible between people. That is where I want to begin when I call a word “code”: with consequence.

**1B — Metaphor**

A compiler is a useful image for speech. It becomes less useful when the image starts pretending to be evidence. I want the analogy to illuminate the act without swallowing the body that performs it.

**2A — Breath**

A mantra is a path a body makes through breath. Tongue, pressure, duration, and listening belong to that path. Meaning does not arrive in an empty room.

**2B — Measurement**

We can study a recitation through pitch, duration, articulation, spectrum, and continuity. A measurement describes a contour. It does not settle the meaning of the tradition that carries it.

**3A — Preservation**

A line can survive because it is remembered in more than one way. Redundancy gives disagreement somewhere to appear. Preservation begins to fail when every copy is allowed to drift together.

**3B — Judgment**

Memory can return a sentence. Judgment has to answer for what we do with it now. The braid is not the meaning.

**4A — Witness**

An archive remembers. A model proposes. A living person remains answerable for the consequence. That distinction matters most at the moment a tool can act.

**4B — Pause**

One practical question for any automated system: where is the pause before consequence? A witness is more than a name in a log. Someone must still be able to notice, refuse, and redirect.

**5A — Ground**

Before the body becomes a diagram, it is the place from which the diagram is read. A map can orient us. It can also teach us to distrust the ground beneath our feet.

**5B — Boundaries**

A limit can support a life until it becomes a verdict on that life. I’m interested in the moment a useful boundary turns into an inherited command.

**6A — Inquiry**

An observation and its explanation are different kinds of commitment. We lose something when doubt about one is treated as permission to believe anything about the other.

**6B — Revision**

What would make you revise the model you care about most? I want that question beside every beautiful explanation I build.

## Upload procedure for each installment

1. Read the complete source and relevant claim ledger; mark traditional interpretation, observation, mathematical model, and contemporary analogy. For empirical claims, verify primary sources before adaptation.
2. Check Published and Drafts for duplicates. Record source slug, source commit/hash, new title, series position, asset, and Substack draft URL in the tracking table below.
3. Prepare the adaptation locally: 900–1800 words where the argument supports it, without deleting necessary evidence or distorting the original. Strip YAML frontmatter, gallery controls, figure IDs, and private processing/source-bridge metadata.
4. Use the publisher's Create control to save a draft. Add title, subtitle, one main image, readable headings, source links, image alt text, and a short glossary where needed. Keep the existing two drafts intact.
5. Preview desktop and mobile. Confirm mathematics, diacritics, captions, links, series navigation, source attribution, and the footer. Do not assume gallery interactions or TeX will survive the editor; use readable text or a checked equation image if needed.
6. Choose audience, email delivery, and schedule explicitly at the publishing step. This plan does not change payment settings or send subscriber emails. Recommended launch policy: free series articles and public Notes; final audience selection remains an editorial decision.
7. After publication, record the public URL and verify it signed out. Only then publish the companion Note that links to it.
8. After two installments, review replies, subscriptions attributable to posts, source-link clicks where available, and read/open trends. Adjust length or pacing against the publication's own baseline; no guaranteed growth targets.
