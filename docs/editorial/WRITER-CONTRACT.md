1. Read the whole selected source and record its hash and actual reading depth. Select subject, audience and genre, then build the compact conceptual brief: question, senses, relationships, source contribution, proposed integration and unresolved difference. A historical or adaptation assignment need not invent a new contribution.
2. Read the writer skill and selected references. Preserve its traditional/empirical/house-model/synthesis/metaphor distinctions in the private claim trace.
3. Draft from the integrated understanding and material. Choose a form suited to the actual question; avoid importing a template's rhetorical shape or a dated belief sequence into every topic.
4. Read the whole source and entire assembled output for semantic fidelity, then reread repaired spans with adjacent context and later dependencies. Follow actors, referents, causal direction, temporal scope, modality, vocabulary senses and ownership of synthesis across paragraphs. Check paragraph development, repeated thoughts, transitions and the actual contribution. Preserve imagery, conceptual wit, human texture and discovery. Compare neighbouring outputs for repeated semantic shapes as well as wording; a zero-findings scan cannot certify this review.
