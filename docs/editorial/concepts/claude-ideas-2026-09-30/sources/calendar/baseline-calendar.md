## Editorial spine

The publication asks how a person reads, inhabits, and changes the systems that shape experience. Computing provides one vocabulary; speech, ritual, geometry, bodily observation, and narrative supply others. The newsletter should move between these vocabularies through a legible series structure, preserving the author's direct clinical-visionary voice from `docs/VOICE.md`.

The unit of coverage is the whole repertoire, not the latest essay. The companion [catalogue](2026-09-30-editorial-catalogue.md) assigns every current blog entry to a primary series; its JSON records source hashes. A six-month calendar samples the complete map, while unscheduled entries remain in the backlog. Coverage does not require emailing every archive entry.

Do not select archive copies when the current source exists. Keep private operational notes and third-party excerpts outside the public copy.

## Drafting and publication contract

Prepare local adaptations first, typically 900–1800 words when the argument fits. Preserve necessary evidence over a word target. Use one clear argument, an embodied opening, source links, at most a compact glossary, one useful image, and a reader question. Retain the distinct travelogue and essay registers. Strip YAML and private processing metadata; check equations, Sanskrit diacritics and image rights rather than assuming the blog layout transfers.

Before any future editor write, refresh Published/Drafts in IAB and check duplication. A local plan does not authorize Substack draft creation, publication, scheduling, Notes or subscriber emails. Once separately authorized, preview on desktop/mobile, select audience/email delivery explicitly, then record and verify the public URL. Never invent future public URLs.

Suggested footer: “Adapted from Synchronocities. Read the complete source: [verified title/link]. Part of [series name]. [Publication map] · [Previous published installment].” Links remain placeholders until verified.

The master dashboard suggests platform and content metrics but contains queries, not observed performance. After four articles, review the publication's actual replies, post-linked subscriptions and available read/open trends against its own baseline. Treat the calendar as an editable operating proposal, with no growth guarantee.
