# What I’m building, and what I’ll write here

*Attention, symbolic systems and the choices inside the tools we make.*

I’m Shesh Iyer. I’m building Tryambakam Noesis, and I want to make more of the work visible while it is taking shape.

A tool always contains a view of the person using it. A notebook assumes something about what deserves to be recorded. A calendar assumes something about what deserves time. An assistant that offers an answer makes a choice about which question it heard.

Those choices interest me. They are present in the interface, in the language around a practice, and in the habits that form around a system.

The Why Chromosome is where I’ll examine them at length. Software, symbolic frameworks, embodied practice and narrative belong here because they give different ways to approach a question. Each needs its own terms and limits. A metaphor can open a view. An explanation needs more than resemblance.

I also want to share the making itself: a design decision, a useful constraint, a passage that changes during revision, or a question that a working tool brings into focus. A project announcement can name what was shipped. A working note can show why one choice mattered.

AI is part of that work. It helps with research, drafting and implementation. I choose what to keep, check what can be checked, and take responsibility for what I publish. The amount a system can generate is a poor guide to how much deserves a reader’s attention.

One current example is the publishing workflow behind these drafts. It prepares articles, Notes and assets, then holds each item for review. The copy, destination and delivery settings belong to the decision. Preparing a post and choosing to release it are separate steps.

That separation matters to the writing as much as it does to the software. It leaves room to ask whether the piece has said something useful, whether its claims are supported, and whether this is the right form for the idea.

The next two essays return to a smaller scale.

“Why Insight Isn’t Change” begins with the choice after a realization. Seeing a pattern does not settle the next request, the next interruption or the next moment of friction. The essay asks what becomes visible when attention moves to that next choice.

“A Record Before an Explanation” offers five fields for an ordinary event: trigger, signal, felt shift, action and story. The aim is to put enough of the scene on paper that an explanation has something to answer to. The fields can be left incomplete. Uncertainty belongs in a record too.

Around those essays, I’ll share shorter notes and examples from the work. On X, that may be a compact design decision or a question. On Instagram, a visual sequence can hold one distinction. A Reddit contribution should meet the discussion already happening there. The longer essay gets the space it needs here.

I’d like this to be a place where making and examining stay close together. You can follow a tool being built, question an assumption, try a small practice, or stay with an essay that asks for a slower reading.

What part of making do you wish people showed more clearly: the decisions, the revisions, the failed assumptions, or the thing that finally became useful?
