# Meaning-preserving examples

These are constructed editorial examples, not quotations, autobiography or prescribed sentence templates. Their purpose is to show the decisions behind a revision. The full candidates test whether those decisions survive an assembled piece.

## A concept before a timeline

Weak: “In July I learned Mercury. In September it became the field. Today it is my operating system.”

Revision: “Mercury can name a metal, a messenger, a planet or an alchemical principle. For this inquiry, the messenger is useful because something must carry meaning between a received impression and an answer.”

The first sentence invents a personal chronology and collapses meanings. The revision identifies senses and owns a limited contribution. Young's list is source material; mediation in this inquiry is the proposed integration. A historical essay could legitimately use a sourced chronology.

## Keep the actual spiritual question

Weak: “Soul is bioelectric coherence. Spirit is the body's information field.”

Revision: “Young distinguishes body, soul and spirit through his Three Primes. I want to keep that distinction available while asking what a person carries into the work, what gives it form, and what can pass through it.”

This is a proposed authorial reading, not adopted canon or a technical translation. Biology would require a different source and claim. The revision preserves an open spiritual inquiry without pretending that precise-sounding replacements establish a mechanism.

## Purposeful negation

Weak: “You are not your role. You are the infinite field behind every role.”

Revision: “The role explains what the room expects. It leaves something unanswered about the person expected to perform it.”

The revision identifies a limit without installing a new universal identity. Neti-neti may continue questioning that limit; it need not end with an alternative doctrine. A genuine philosophical contrast remains available.

## An anti-list is a reason, not a search-and-replace table

Weak: “The healing journey becomes metabolic integration.”

Revision: “If the speaker describes recovery, keep recovery. If the sentence promises a health effect, establish the effect or narrow the claim. Renaming it does neither.”

A phrase can be generic, but its repair depends on what it claims. The new wording must preserve the intended sense. “Metabolic” introduces a biological burden; it cannot be used as decorative rigour.

## Whole-output fidelity

Weak local repair: paragraph2 calls a comparison metaphorical; paragraph5 concludes that the same comparison proves how consciousness works.

Revision decision: reread the whole argument, narrow the conclusion to the interpretation actually developed, and check its next sentence and final invitation. Do not merely add a disclaimer to paragraph2.

An adjacent-context review catches repeated or dangling paragraphs introduced by surgery. A whole-output review catches an analogy becoming ontology later. Both concern meaning; neither can be certified by zero banned words.
