# Recognition needs room for correction

A familiar beginning can steal the end of a sentence. You recognize the pattern, prepare the reply, and stop receiving before the new information arrives. Recognition needs room for correction.

Imagine: “I’m concerned about the deadline because…” You prepare a defense. The sentence ends: “…the reviewer will be away.” Your answer belonged to an earlier conversation. This is a hypothetical example, not a reported event.

Keep three things distinct: the words heard, the meaning you expected, and the action you are about to take. A remembered pattern can guide an expectation without deciding what the current sentence permits.

A revision window is the interval before an interpretation becomes an act. I’m proposing the term as an instrument: let the reason arrive, ask what could change the decision, then reply.

The counterexample matters. Urgent action may come first. Repeated boundary violations may warrant refusal. Listening longer is useful when more information could change the judgment; it need not become endless availability.
