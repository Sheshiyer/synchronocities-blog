Crescent geometry on an island famous for its full moon.

The irony wasn't lost. Koh Phangan — pilgrimage site for thousands seeking the full moon's promise of illumination, communal ecstasy, electric connection. The card I read beside that promise was the Moon: a vocabulary of borrowed light and uncertain seeing.

In the Thoth deck, the Moon shows a corridor between two towers, a scarab pushing the sun disk through the underworld. The water at the bottom teems with hidden life. Nothing on this card is what it appears. I read the towers as pylons of a gate, the corridor as a demand to walk while seeing remains uncertain.

The manomaya kosha — the mental sheath — is the Moon's native territory. This is where projection lives. Where the mind generates narrative from fragments, where meaning is manufactured faster than reality can deliver raw data. The Moon doesn't lie. It reveals how much of what you see is *you* — your filters, your assumptions, your inherited patterns of interpretation.

Koh Phangan delivered this lesson through geometry. The crescent — not the full circle. Partial light. The partial light made me notice how much I was already adding to what I could see.

Refraction is not distortion. A prism doesn't corrupt white light. It reveals the spectrum that was always contained within it. The Moon refracts your perception to show you its hidden spectrum — the wavelengths of assumption, expectation, and inherited meaning-making that color every raw experience before consciousness can receive it unfiltered.

The question isn't whether the Moon deceives. The question is whether you can distinguish between what the Moon shows and what you project onto its silver screen.

---

**Reading through Kha-Ba-La:** Kha names the capacity to notice a projection. Ba is the body on the island receiving light and sensation. La is the inherited expectation through which those sensations become a story. The lens gives me a way to return to the crescent without requiring the optical image to explain perception literally.
