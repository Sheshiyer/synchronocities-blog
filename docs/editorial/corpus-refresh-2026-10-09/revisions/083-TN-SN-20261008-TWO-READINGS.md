A later reading can change what an encounter means without changing what happened.

Imagine a sentence you first read as refusal and later read as care. Keep the sentence, the first reading, and the later reading beside one another. If the later account quietly replaces the earlier one, the account loses its own movement.

In The Lighted Clearing, I separate the source from working memory and present judgment. Here that distinction becomes a writing question: what must remain available for an interpretation to change honestly?

A later reading should leave the earlier one visible enough to surprise you. What have you reread lately that your first explanation could not hold?
