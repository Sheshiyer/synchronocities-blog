# What I’m building, and what I’ll write here

*Attention, symbolic systems and the choices inside the tools we make.*

I’m Shesh Iyer. I’m building Tryambakam Noesis, where software, symbolic inquiry and embodied practice keep finding themselves at the same table. They do not always agree about the furniture.

A notebook decides what gets a field. A calendar gives some things a square and leaves others floating around the edges of the week. An assistant answers the question it thinks it heard. Each tool carries a view of the person using it, sometimes buried deep enough that the interface can pass it off as common sense.

Those choices interest me: what a form makes visible, what a useful model leaves out, what happens when an interpretation acquires a button marked “Send.” The Why Chromosome gives me room to follow them at length.

Software, symbolic frameworks, narrative and bodily practice bring different kinds of attention to a question. Their meetings can be fruitful and ridiculous. A metaphor may open a door; the technical claim on the other side still needs evidence of its own. I want room for both the imaginative leap and the awkward question it has to answer on landing.

You’ll see some of the making here too: a design decision, a constraint that proved useful, a passage that changed in revision, a working tool that made its own assumptions harder to ignore. Announcing a project tells you what was shipped. Showing a decision lets you examine something about how it came to be.

AI participates in research, drafting and implementation. I choose what to keep, check what can be checked and remain responsible for what I publish. Generating more pages is now remarkably easy. Giving a page enough attention to deserve someone else’s time is still work.

The publishing workflow itself brings that question into focus. It prepares copy and assets for review, with the intended destination and delivery settings attached. An attractive preview cannot decide whether a claim is supported or a piece belongs in public. That choice needs a person willing to read what is actually there.

Two companion essays approach the inquiry through ordinary scenes. “Why Insight Isn’t Change” asks what happens after a realization, when another request or interruption arrives. “A Record Before an Explanation” offers five fields—trigger, signal, felt shift, action and story—for keeping an event available while its interpretation moves. A field can remain blank. The notebook is allowed to know less than its owner would like.

Shorter notes can follow the work across X, Instagram and Reddit, each with a form suited to the place. A visual sequence can make one distinction visible; a contribution to a discussion needs to meet the discussion already happening. Here, a longer essay can take the space its question needs.

I want making and examining to stay close enough to inconvenience each other. A tool should be able to disturb the theory that produced it. An essay should survive a reader arriving with a better example.

What part of making do you wish people showed more clearly: a decision, a revision, an assumption that failed, or the thing that finally became useful?
