**Exact title:** What belongs in the original record, and what belongs in a later reading?

**Exact body:**

```text
I’m developing this question in my writing for Tryambakam Noesis: how can an account remain faithful to an event while letting its meaning change?

Consider an imagined conversation. Someone begins a sentence you have heard before. You prepare the familiar reply. Then they finish differently.

There are now at least three things worth keeping: the words they actually used, the ending you expected, and what you noticed when the expectation failed. If you rewrite the first account to make it agree with the later one, you lose the difference that taught you something.

My proposed distinction is between preserving an encounter and preserving an explanation. Keep the original words when you have them. Put an uncertain recollection in its own terms. Add the later reading beside it, with a date, rather than quietly replacing the earlier account.

This is a writing question, not a demand to record every conversation. Sometimes there is no reliable record. That absence belongs in the account too.

When you revisit something you wrote, what do you keep unchanged so that you can see how your interpretation moved?
```
