## Whitehead's Process Philosophy and Bergson's Creative Evolution

Sheldrake's architecture has philosophical antecedents. The most important are Alfred North Whitehead's **process philosophy** and Henri Bergson's **creative evolution**.

Whitehead's *Process and Reality* (1929) replaces the substance ontology of Western philosophy with an ontology of **occasions of experience** — momentary events that prehend (grasp) their predecessors and synthesize them into new actualities. Every actual entity is a process of concrescence: a coming-together of the past into a novel unity. The past is not dead. It is actively taken up into every present moment.

Morphic fields are Whiteheadian societies — structured aggregates of occasions that inherit a common character from their predecessors. The field is the pattern of inheritance. Morphic resonance is the **vector of prehension** — the direct influence of the past on the present, mediated not by efficient causation through space-time but by the intrinsic relatedness of occasions. Whitehead called this relation "the solidarity of the universe."

Bergson's *Creative Evolution* (1907) distinguished between the intellect (which analyzes the already-formed) and intuition (which participates in the forming). The intellect sees fixed laws because it freezes the flow. The *élan vital* — the vital impulse — is the continuous creation of novelty. Bergson argued that evolution is not the mechanical unfolding of pre-existing possibilities but the genuine creation of new forms. Morphic resonance is the mechanism by which this creation is structured. The fields canalize the élan without fixing it.

Both Whitehead and Bergson recognized that the mechanistic paradigm cannot accommodate time as genuine becoming. For both, the past is real and active. For both, the present is the creative synthesis of that past. Sheldrake's theory is the empirical specification of what they described philosophically.
