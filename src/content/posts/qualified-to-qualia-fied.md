## The Explanatory Gap

The philosopher Joseph Levine named this the *explanatory gap*: the difference between explaining a phenomenon in functional terms and explaining why there is something it is like to experience that phenomenon. The gap is not informational — we could have a complete neural map of every process involved in seeing red and still not have bridged the gap between the neural description and the felt experience.

```
Third-person data (science):
  Input:   Light at 700nm wavelength
  Process: Retinal cone activation → optic nerve signal
           → V1 processing → V4 color processing
  Output:  Report "I see red"
  → Complete functional description.
  → No address for experience itself.

First-person data (direct):
  Input:   "I am seeing red. Not the concept of red.
           Not the neural correlate of red. The red itself."
  Process: Irreducible. Cannot be decomposed.
  Output:  "This is what seeing red is like."
  → No third-person verification possible.
  → The only person who can validate this is the experiencer.
```

The gap is not a problem to be solved. It is the shape of the territory.

## The Protocol for Direct Knowing

The traditions do not merely assert that direct knowing exists. They provide repeatable protocols for accessing it.

The protocol for direct knowing has the following general structure:

```
1. Suspend conceptual activity.
   (Pratyahara — withdraw attention from external input.)

2. Stabilize attention on a single object of awareness.
   (Dharana — the mind held at a point rather than distributed.)

3. Allow the distinction between knower and known to dissolve.
   (Dhyana — the subject-object boundary becomes permeable.)

4. Recognize that the awareness that was attending to the object
   and the object of attention are the same awareness.
   (Samadhi — the witness recognizes itself as the witnessed.)
```
