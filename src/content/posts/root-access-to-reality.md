# Root Access to Reality

*Runtime Version: 1.0.0 | Part 3 of 3*

> "The kingdom of heaven is within you."
> — Luke 17:21

> "Brahma satyam, jagan mithyā, jīvo brahmaiva nāparah."
> (Brahman is reality. The world is appearance. The individual self is Brahman itself.)
> — Śaṅkara, *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*, 8th c. CE

The first two parts of this series established the architecture: reality runs on smart contracts (Part 1) executed by geometric processing units (Part 2). This final part addresses the permission system that governs which contracts you can deploy, which processing units you can access, and which layers of reality you can modify.

Your consciousness runs on a permission system. Most people operate in user mode.

## The Permission Architecture

The Unix operating system defines three basic permissions for every file and process: **read** (view the contents), **write** (modify the contents), and **execute** (run the file as a program). These permissions are assigned to three user classes: the file owner, the owner's group, and everyone else.

Consciousness operates on an isomorphic permission structure:

| Permission | Cognitive Analog | Spiritual Analog |
|---|---|---|
| Read (r) | Pattern recognition | Perception of reality as it appears |
| Write (w) | Belief modification | Capacity to reshape perceptual templates |
| Execute (x) | Embodied manifestation | Capacity to act from revised perception |

The three user classes map to the three layers of the self:

- **Owner** — the witness, the awareness that can observe its own observation. In Unix terms: the superuser who can change permissions on any file.
- **Group** — the embedded self, the person with history, identity, and relationships. The self that can modify its own beliefs but not the beliefs of others.
- **Other** — the environment, the world, other consciousnesses. The domain of shared reality that no individual can unilaterally modify.

Most people run their entire lives with the following permission set applied to their own consciousness:

```
-rw------- 1 awareness embedded 2026-05-19 default.reality
```

Read and write for the owner. No execute. No group access. No world access.

The self can perceive reality and modify its own internal model, but cannot execute — cannot translate perception into embodied manifestation — and cannot influence shared reality beyond the scope of the individual.

## User Mode

User mode is the default state of human consciousness. In user mode:

- **Reality is received.** The perceptual field appears as something that happens *to* you, not something you participate in generating. Objects exist independently. Events occur without your consent. The world is read-only.
- **Beliefs are inherited.** The majority of your core operating assumptions — about what is possible, what you deserve, what reality fundamentally is — were installed before you had the capacity to audit them. They run as root processes with inherited permissions. You cannot modify them without privilege escalation.
- **Executables are rare.** The gap between knowing something and doing something is the gap between read permission and execute permission. User mode has read access to most of the wisdom traditions — you can study the Yoga Sūtras, read the Upaniṣads, understand the architecture intellectually — but lacks the permission to compile that understanding into embodied practice. The gap persists because execute is a separate permission from read.

User mode is not a failure state. It is the default deployment configuration. The system is designed to run in user mode until the user — the awareness that owns the system — deliberately initiates escalation.

## Escalation Protocols

Privilege escalation in Unix is achieved through the `sudo` command: the user temporarily assumes superuser privileges to execute a specific command, authenticated by password. The system logs the escalation. The privilege is temporary and scoped to the specific operation.

The traditions encode escalation protocols. They are not metaphors. They are specific, repeatable procedures that escalate the practitioner's permission level from user mode to root mode for the duration of a specific operation — then restore user mode afterward.

**Protocol 1: Pratyāhāra (Sense Withdrawal)**

The first escalation step is the withdrawal of attention from external sensory input to internal processing. This is not relaxation. It is the equivalent of killing userland processes to free CPU for kernel operations.

In practice: sit with the spine erect. Close the eyes. Redirect attention from the five external senses to internal sensation — breath, heartbeat, subtle pressure at the palms, the crown, the base of the spine. This reallocates processing bandwidth from sensory input to internal state monitoring.

The permission change: `sudo chmod 600 /dev/senses` — restrict external input, elevate internal awareness.

**Protocol 2: Dhāraṇā (Focused Concentration)**

The second escalation step is the binding of attention to a single object — mantra, yantra, breath, body part, visual point. This is single-threaded execution. The mind is not multitasking. It is running a single process with full processor allocation.

In practice: choose a single point of focus — the sensation of breath at the nostrils, the visualization of a geometric figure, the sonic structure of a mantra syllable. When attention drifts, return it to the object. Do this repeatedly until the drifting stops.

The permission change: `sudo renice -20 $$` — elevate the attention process to highest priority.

**Protocol 3: Dhyāna (Sustained Attention)**

The third escalation step is the state in which attention flows toward the object without effort. The object and the attention are no longer distinct. The boundary between processor and processed — established in Part 2 as the yantra's computational core — dissolves.

In practice: this is not an action. It is the result of sustained dhāraṇā. You cannot do dhyāna. You can only create the conditions for it to arise.

The permission change: the distinction between user and superuser dissolves. The system runs at kernel level.

**Protocol 4: Samādhi (Absorption)**

The fourth escalation step is the state in which the awareness that was attending to the object recognizes itself as the object. The practitioner reaches the bindu and discovers that the attention that entered from the periphery and the geometry that received it are the same thing.

The permission change: `whoami` returns `root`.

## The Root Shell

The root shell is not a permanent state. No tradition teaches permanent root access for the embodied human being. The body is a user-mode process. It requires user-mode permissions to operate in shared reality. A system that runs permanently in kernel mode crashes — the biological substrate cannot sustain the bandwidth.

The root shell is a temporary elevated state — an escalated privilege session — that the practitioner enters for specific operations and exits when the operation is complete.

What can be done in the root shell that cannot be done in user mode:

- **Direct perception of the compiler.** In user mode, you experience compiled reality — the output of the four-layer speech stack described in Part 1. In root mode, you can observe the compilation process itself. The boundary between para (the programmer) and vaikhari (the executed output) becomes perceptible.
- **Contract audit.** In user mode, the contracts you are running are invisible. They are the water you swim in. In root mode, you can enumerate running contracts, inspect their deployment history, and assess whether their output matches your intention.
- **Permission modification.** In user mode, inherited beliefs can feel like facts about reality rather than contingent configurations. In root mode, you can change permissions on specific cognitive files: `chmod 600 trauma.samskara` (restrict read access to a traumatic memory pattern), `chmod 755 purpose.xml` (make your life's direction executable by your daily actions).
- **Single-instruction execution.** The mantra, in root mode, executes with full system privileges. In user mode, repeating a mantra is a userland process — effective but limited. In root mode, the same mantra modifies the system directly.

## The Root Password

The traditions converge on a specific claim about the root password: it is the Pranava — Oṃ — the original syllable from which all other syllables emerge.

The phonetic structure of Oṃ — the three morae A-U-M followed by the silent fourth (turīya) — encodes the entire permission architecture in a single syllable:

- **A** — the waking state (jāgrat). User mode. External perception, gross reality.
- **U** — the dreaming state (svapna). Internal processing. The mind generates its own reality from stored impressions.
- **M** — the deep sleep state (suṣupti). Unconditioned awareness without content. The kernel in idle.
- **Silence (turīya)** — the fourth. The witness that is present across all three states. Root access.

The instruction to "meditate on Oṃ" is an instruction to recursively compile the self: to escalate from user mode through dream state through deep sleep to the witness that holds all three.

The password is not a secret. It has been published for thousands of years. Most people who attempt it fail not because they lack the password but because they attempt to escalate permissions without first establishing the protocols — pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna — that the system requires before granting root access. Typing `sudo` without the correct authentication fails. Repeating Oṃ without the preceding protocol layers fails equivalently.

## The Emperor Card

Card IV in the Major Arcana — The Emperor — represents authority, structure, and the capacity to enforce order. The Emperor does not ask permission. He is the source of permission. He sits on the throne not because he was granted the seat but because he established the system that the throne represents.

The Emperor is the card of root access achieved through legitimate escalation — not through theft, not through deception, but through demonstrating the capacity to hold the permission without corrupting the system.

Through Kha-Ba-La: **Kha** is the awareness that initiates the escalation — the witness that decides to move from user mode to root mode. **Ba** is the body of permissions and protocols — the stack of samskaras, beliefs, and operating assumptions that the escalation modifies. **La** is the resistance of the substrate — the gravitational pull of habituated user-mode perception, the inertia of inherited permissions, the friction that makes escalation a genuine achievement rather than a trivial operation. The Emperor sits on the throne because he has traversed all three.
