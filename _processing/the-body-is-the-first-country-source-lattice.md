# Source lattice — The Body Is the First Country

## Reader-facing primary sources

- **Bhagavad Gītā 13.2** — kṣetra / kṣetrajña distinction. The article reports this as a traditional source claim, not empirical proof.
- **Phenomenology Online, “Existentiality”** — summary of Ludwig Binswanger’s Umwelt, Mitwelt, and Eigenwelt as surrounding, shared, and self-world coordinates. Used as a modern phenomenological reference, not as a Vedic equivalent.
- **Ṛg Veda 10.90, Puruṣa Sūkta** — body/cosmic-order imagery. Used as historical-traditional material, not as a modern anatomy claim.
- **Atharva Veda 12.1, Bhūmi Sūkta** — Earth as ground, bearer, and relational field. Used as attributed hymn material.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.2** — recurring yuga framing. Used as traditional cosmology, not chronological human history.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.3** — longer avatar enumeration and the claim that incarnations are innumerable.
- **Jayadeva, Gīta Govinda** — tenfold poetic avatar arrangement; used to qualify the popular Daśāvatāra list without making a reception-history claim.
- **OpenStax Biology 2e, 5.2** — membrane transport and selective permeability. Used for the biological cell claim.

## Explicit Noesis syntheses

1. Four yugas as conditions of relation rather than as a universal historical calendar.
2. Daśāvatāra as forms answering conditions.
3. Body–land–mind as a cross-scale interpretive model.
4. Biological, social, prison, and contemplative “cell” as distinct boundary cases.
5. Kha–Ba–La as a diagnostic for whether a limit is witnessed, embodied, and materially answered.
